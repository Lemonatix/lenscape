Physics: thin lens in the weak-field approximation, \(\beta = \theta - \theta_\mathrm{E}^2/\theta\) (point mass) or \(\beta = \theta - \alpha_\mathrm{SIE}(\theta)\) (elliptical isothermal galaxy, Keeton 2001; for \(q = 1\), \(|\alpha| = \theta_\mathrm{E}\)) with \(\theta_\mathrm{E}^2 = (4GM/c^2)\,D_\mathrm{ds}/(D_\mathrm{d}\,D_\mathrm{s})\). A slower \(c\) strengthens the deflection as \(1/c^2\). Distances are Euclidean (\(D_\mathrm{ds} = D_\mathrm{s} - D_\mathrm{d}\)); the cosmologically correct calculation lives in the Python part of lenscape. For very strong fields (close to the black hole) the approximation is only qualitative. Everything is computed per pixel on the GPU (WebGL2). To embed in other pages: index.html?embed.
